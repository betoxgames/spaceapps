# Space Papers UX review · 15 November 2026

This pass prioritizes the document-review experience, with the supplied lunar room, physical papers, manual and stamps remaining the visual and interaction foundation. Resource rules, costs, probabilities, outcomes and NASA source material are preserved.

## Findings and implementation

| Area | Observed problem | Improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small screens | A full-screen warning blocked the entire game below 1000px width or 600px height, including its menu and accessibility options. | Menus and dialogs use a compact layout. The physical desk keeps readable dimensions and can be scrolled, with a dismissible navigation hint. |
| First shift | The title screen did not explain the essential comparison loop or distinguish practice from a real shift. | A concise introduction, a clearly marked training entry and contextual next-step guidance explain the task without revealing the correct decision. |
| Document recovery | Overlapping sheets were recoverable only by keyboard shortcuts, which were easy to miss. | Named document controls expose recovery directly and retain the physical paper arrangement. |
| Paper layout | Life-support and propulsion sheets overflowed their fixed height; notes collided with footer text. | Revised internal spacing and type keep all requested resources, notes and footers inside each sheet. |
| Manual | Both emergency-reference pages clipped content; several NASA pages clipped their final lines. | Page spacing and reading areas expose the complete content. Page controls keep their focus when the book rerenders. |
| Dialogs | Dialogs had no accessible name; background controls remained focusable. Tab could leave the menu when seed replay was collapsed. | Named dialogs, an inert background and focus containment protect keyboard and assistive-technology navigation. |
| Reading and returning | Manual and inventory rerenders discarded the active button; closing an overlay could leave the player without a clear focus position. | Focus is preserved or restored to the relevant control, document or tool. |
| Stamp interaction | Escape could interrupt the whole shift while the player was trying to cancel an object action. Stamp selection and eligible targets lacked persistent state feedback. | Escape cancels the current action first. Selected stamps and valid targets show clear feedback while resources are spent only when a decision is committed. |
| Pause and motion | Reduced-motion coverage and transitions needed consistency across physical objects, overlays and the cinema. | Motion preferences cover the interface; active time remains paused while pause dialogs are open. |
| Visual hierarchy | Small labels, low-contrast focus rings and inconsistent button states made the workstation harder to scan. | A shared palette, stronger type hierarchy, layered focus indicators and consistent selected, hover, pressed and disabled states improve readability. |
| End of turn | Report and final-evaluation navigation needed the same accessibility and layout treatment as the menu. | Reports and evaluation dialogs are named, keyboard reachable and responsive; unavailable log-page actions are disabled. |

The baseline inspection measured actual content overflow at 1280 × 720: life-support paper 425/392px scroll/client height, propulsion paper 419/392px, emergency manual pages 356/310px and several NASA pages 322/310px. These are layout measurements in the unscaled document coordinate system, rather than estimates from screenshots.

## Verification

`tests/ux-browser.mjs` exercises menus and options at 360 × 740, 390 × 844, 768 × 1024, 960 × 540, 1280 × 720, 1440 × 900 and 1920 × 1080. It checks accessible dialog names, keyboard focus containment, compact touch targets, scroll access to credits, saved preferences, paper-content fit, all eleven paginated manual spreads, focus restoration, pause navigation, and the emergency → report → final-evaluation path.

The existing domain, clock, desk, camera, visitor, character, training, incident and direct-file offline suites remain relevant. The dedicated desk-accessibility suite covers cancellation, stamp feedback and physical-object input. Generated screenshots are saved under the ignored `test-results/` directory for visual review.

The dedicated UX suite passed after implementation: all seven screen sizes, focus checks, preference persistence, manual pages, document fit, compact document recovery and the final-evaluation flow completed without uncaught browser errors. Screenshots of compact options, the recovered paper and the desktop evaluation were also visually reviewed. Final results for the broader regression suites and regenerated standalone offline build are recorded in the delivery summary; the existence of a test is not itself a passing result.

## Remaining validation

- Conduct observed sessions with first-time players, especially children aged 7–12: measure whether they find the manual, compare all five resources, distinguish selecting a stamp from committing it, and understand the emergency contribution.
- Validate on physical touch devices and with VoiceOver/NVDA. Browser keyboard checks do not substitute for assistive-technology or motor-accessibility testing.
- The compact desk preserves the spatial paper mechanic through scrolling. It does not claim a fully redesigned one-screen phone workstation; a landscape tablet or desktop remains more comfortable for simultaneous document comparison.
- Validate competition-specific presentation requirements and final art/font/audio permissions separately. This review does not establish prize eligibility or claim human usability results.
